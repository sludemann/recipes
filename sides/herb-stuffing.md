# Herb Stuffing

Serves 8 to 10. See Notes for the Stove Top version.

## Ingredients

- 1 pound crusty white bread, torn into roughly 1-inch pieces
- 3/4 cup unsalted butter, plus more for the dish
- 2 1/2 cups chopped yellow onion
- 1 1/2 cups thinly sliced celery
- 1/2 cup chopped flat-leaf parsley
- 2 tablespoons chopped fresh sage
- 1 tablespoon chopped fresh rosemary
- 1 tablespoon chopped fresh thyme
- 2 teaspoons Diamond Crystal kosher salt or 1 teaspoon Morton kosher salt
- 1 teaspoon ground black pepper
- 2 1/2 cups low-sodium chicken broth, divided
- 2 large eggs

## Instructions

1. Spread the torn bread on a rimmed sheet pan. Dry it at 250°F for about an hour, turning occasionally, until crisp all the way through. Let it cool in a large mixing bowl. Butter a 9-by-13-inch baking dish.
2. Melt the butter in a skillet over medium-high heat. Cook the onion and celery for about 10 minutes, stirring often, until soft and lightly browned at the edges. Toss with the bread, herbs, salt, and pepper. Add 1 1/4 cups broth, toss gently, and let the mixture cool until lukewarm.
3. Increase the oven temperature to 350°F. Beat the eggs with the remaining 1 1/4 cups broth and fold into the bread. Spread the mixture loosely in the baking dish and cover with foil.
4. Bake covered for 35 to 40 minutes. Remove the foil and bake until the top is crisp and browned and the center reaches 165°F. Start checking after 25 minutes uncovered.

## Notes

### Stove Top version

- Use **one 6-ounce box of Stove Top Turkey Stuffing Mix and 10 ounces of crusty white bread**, weighed before drying. Dry only the torn bread in step 1. Add the dry mix to the bowl with the cooled bread before adding the vegetables and broth in step 2. The broth in the recipe hydrates both.
- Use **1/2 cup unsalted butter**, keep the same amounts of onion, celery, parsley, and pepper, and halve the sage, rosemary, and thyme. Omit the added salt and use unsalted chicken broth since the box is already seasoned. Keep both eggs.
- Start with the same **2 1/2 cups of broth** and let the mixture sit for 5 minutes after adding the egg and broth in step 3. If the bread still has dry centers, fold in more broth 1/4 cup at a time, up to 1/2 cup extra. The bread should be evenly damp with no broth pooling in the bowl. Bake as directed in step 4.
