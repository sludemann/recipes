# Pie Crust

Makes one 9-inch deep-dish bottom crust. Double the recipe for two pies.

## Ingredients

- 1 2/3 cups (200 g) all-purpose flour, plus more for rolling
- 1 tablespoon (12 g) granulated sugar
- 1/2 teaspoon fine salt
- 8 tablespoons (113 g) unsalted butter, cold and cut into 1/2-inch cubes
- 2 tablespoons (about 25 g) vegetable shortening, cold
- 1 teaspoon apple cider vinegar
- 3 to 5 tablespoons ice water

## Instructions

1. Whisk the flour, sugar, and salt together in a large bowl. Work in the shortening with a pastry cutter or your fingertips until the mixture is sandy. Add the butter and cut it in until most pieces are pea-sized, with a few larger, flat pieces still visible.
2. Stir the vinegar into 3 tablespoons of ice water. Drizzle it over the flour mixture while tossing with a fork. Add more ice water 1 teaspoon at a time until a handful holds together when squeezed. The dough should still look a little shaggy.
3. Gather the dough into a disk and wrap it. Refrigerate for at least 1 hour.
4. Roll the dough on a lightly floured surface to about 1/8 inch thick and 13 to 14 inches across, depending on the depth of the dish. Lift it into a 9-inch deep-dish pie plate and ease it into the corners. Trim to a 3/4-inch overhang, tuck the edge under itself, and crimp.
5. Refrigerate the shaped crust for 30 minutes. Bake it according to the pie recipe.

## Notes

- For [Pumpkin Pie](pumpkin-pie.md), par-bake the chilled shell with parchment and weights as directed. For [Pecan Pie](pecan-pie.md), start with the chilled, unbaked shell.
- This formula takes the sugar and mixed fats from #1 and the cold-water method from #2. The butter and shortening together weigh about 69% as much as the flour, between #1 at roughly 45% and #2 at 80%. Butter provides most of the flavor, and the shortening helps the crimp hold its shape. A little vinegar carries over from #1. In King Arthur’s test, its clearest benefit was slowing the graying of raw dough during storage. - #3 uses egg and crème fraîche to bind the dough. This crust uses a measured flour weight and adds ice water gradually so you can adjust the dough by feel. [Egg-and-crème-fraîche pâte brisée](https://www.regensblog.com/2009/04/05/pate-brisee-pie-crust/)
