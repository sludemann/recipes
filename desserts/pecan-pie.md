# Pecan Pie

## Ingredients

- 1 unbaked 9-inch deep-dish pie crust
- 2 cups pecan halves, divided
- 4 large eggs
- 1 cup light corn syrup
- 1/3 cup packed brown sugar
- 1 tablespoon unsulfured molasses
- 1/4 cup (30 g) all-purpose flour
- 1 tablespoon vanilla extract
- 1 teaspoon fine salt
- 6 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted and cooled slightly

## Instructions

1. Heat the oven to 350°F with a rack in the lower third. Fit the crust into a 9-inch deep-dish pie plate if needed, crimp the edge, and refrigerate it while you make the filling.
2. Coarsely chop 1 cup of the pecans and scatter them in the crust. Reserve the remaining halves for the top.
3. Whisk the brown sugar, flour, and salt together, breaking up any lumps. Whisk in the eggs until smooth, then whisk in the corn syrup, molasses, vanilla, and melted butter just until combined.
4. Pour the filling over the chopped pecans and arrange the reserved halves on top. Set the pie plate on a rimmed baking sheet.
5. Bake for about 50 to 65 minutes, checking at 45 minutes. The pie is ready when the edges are set and the center gives a slight wobble when nudged. If using a thermometer, aim for about 200°F in the center. Shield the crust edge with foil if it browns before the filling sets.
6. Cool on a wire rack for about 2 hours to let the filling set. Refrigerate if serving later.

## Notes

- The flour adds body as its starch swells during baking, alongside the eggs, which set the filling. Keep the full 1/4 cup for a firmer slice. For a softer, more syrupy center, try 2 tablespoons (15 g) with the same four eggs. [Starch gelatinization](https://asbe.org/article/starch-gelatinization/) · [How eggs set custards](https://annex.exploratorium.edu/cooking/icooks/article_5-03.html)
- The published recipes use different amounts of flour. King Arthur uses 1/4 cup in a brown sugar and milk filling. Shirley Rooney uses 2 tablespoons per cup of corn syrup, and Karo's classic filling sets with three eggs and no flour. The texture guidance for this four-egg formula is an estimate.
